## 📋 Prerequisites

- **Main camera**: Phone (iPhone 13+ / flagship Android) or mirrorless (Sony ZV-E10 II, Fujifilm X-S20)
- **Overhead arm**: Ulanzi VL-160 / Godox LT80 / Manfrotto 244 magic arm (**critical, don't skimp**)
- **Lighting**: Two LED panels (Godox LEDP120 / Ulanzi VL-49 / Aputure Amaran 60d)
- **Audio**: Lav mic (DJI Mic Mini / Rode Wireless GO II / Audio-Technica AT2020 USB)
- **Extras**: Dark cloth mat, mini tripod (for detail cam), magnetic parts tray
- A device to disassemble (old phone, keyboard, router — anything works)

**Budget tiers:**
- Entry ($200) = phone + Ulanzi arm + 2 lights + lav mic
- Mid-tier ($1200) = ZV-E10 II + magic arm + 2× Amaran 60d + Rode NTG

## Step 1: Clear Desk + Dark Mat (0-10 min)

Reflections and clutter are the two killers of teardown video quality. Fix them first.

**Cleaning principles:**

- Remove everything not needed for the shoot (cups, sticky notes, papers)
- **Avoid white/glossy surfaces** — they blow out metal reflections
- Lay down a **dark grey or black cloth mat** (60×40cm minimum) — same as iFixit uses
- If screws are involved, grab a **magnetic parts tray** ($5-15 from Wowstick or iFixit)

**Reserve lighting space:**
Leave 30cm clear on each side of the desk for the light stands. Decide which corner the overhead arm will clamp to.

**Avoid:**

- ❌ Glass desks: create two giant reflection hotspots
- ❌ Wood grain desks: too visually busy, steals attention
- ❌ Bright white desks: overexposed metal + white balance nightmare

## Step 2: Overhead Rig + Framing (10-25 min)

The overhead is the **hero shot** — viewers spend 80% of the video looking at it.

**Height setup:**

- Camera lens **45-60cm** above the surface (45cm for phones/PCBs, 60cm+ for keyboards/PCs)
- Lens must be **perfectly vertical** (use a bubble level app, or check the four corners are equidistant)
- Clamp the arm **firmly**; wiggle for 10 seconds to confirm no drift

**Composition rules:**

1. **Center the device**, leave 15-20% breathing room (subtitles/annotations will need edge space)
2. **Hands enter from the bottom or sides**, never directly blocking the device
3. Key actions (unscrewing, prying) happen in the **exact center** of frame

**Camera settings:**

```text
Resolution: 4K30 or 1080p60
Frame rate: 30fps (standard) / 60fps (for slow-mo cutaways)
White balance: Manual 5600K (match your LED panels)
Focus: Manual, locked (autofocus will hunt when hands move)
```

**iPhone users:** Use a pro camera app (Halide, Blackmagic Camera) to lock focus and exposure. The stock app will drift.

<Answer>My hand enters from the lower third, the device sits center-upper, and there's margin on all sides</Answer>
</Checkpoint>

## Step 3: Two-Point Soft Lighting (25-35 min)

Metal reflections are the hardest problem in teardown videography. **Hard light = disaster. Soft light = salvation.**

**45° two-light setup (industry standard):**

```text
                Overhead Camera
                       │
                       ▼
    ╔═══════╗       ┌─────┐       ╔═══════╗
    ║ Key A ║ ────► │ Part│ ◄──── ║ Fill B║
    ║ 45°   ║       └─────┘       ║ 45°   ║
    ╚═══════╝                     ╚═══════╝
     Key light                     Fill light
     100% power                    60% power
```

**Critical settings:**

- Both lights **must have diffusion** (softbox, umbrella, or diffusion cloth) — bare LED = dead spots
- Key light (either side) at **100%**, fill on the opposite side at **60%** to lift shadows
- Lights at **45° downward angle** from horizontal, high enough to stay out of frame
- Color temperature locked at **5600K daylight** — both lights must **match** (mixed temps = half yellow half blue)

**No softbox? Emergency options:**

1. Tape a sheet of white A4 paper in front of each light (DIY diffusion)
2. **Bounce light off a white ceiling** (only works if your ceiling is actually white)
3. Shoot near a window and use a single LED for fill

**Verification test:**
Place a coin or screw in frame center. If you see **sharp bright spots or hard shadows**, your light is still too hard. Ideal: metal surfaces show **smooth gradients**, not glare.

<Checkpoint>
Do metal parts show **no obvious hotspots**? Are shadows **soft-edged** rather than sharp?
<Answer>Yes, the diffusion softened everything — metal has even highlights instead of blinding glare</Answer>
</Checkpoint>

## Step 4: Detail Cam + Independent Audio (35-45 min)

The second camera captures the details. In editing, cutaways to this angle make pacing sing.

**Detail cam setup:**

- Use a **mini tripod** (or Gorillapod) placed on the desk itself
- **20-30cm** from the device at a **30-45° angle**
- Phone or secondary camera at 1080p60 or 4K30
- Focus locked on **where hands will operate** (e.g. the screw head)

**What this angle captures:**

- Screws dropping into the parts tray
- Hand movements when prying clips
- Lifting out ribbon cables, screens, batteries — the "reveal" moments
- The final reassembly click

**Independent audio setup:**

⚠️ Camera mics are essentially useless — too far, too noisy, teardown sounds get muddled. **A separate mic is non-negotiable.**

**Option A (voiceover-heavy):** Lav mic clipped to chest, 20cm from mouth

```text
DJI Mic Mini    — $100, wireless, one-button noise reduction
Rode Wireless GO II — $250, dual channels, pro-grade
```

**Option B (teardown sounds first):** Desktop shotgun aimed at the work area

```text
Audio-Technica AT2020 USB  — $100, plug-and-play to laptop
Rode VideoMic NTG          — $200, on-camera shotgun
```

**Option C (budget hack):** Use a second phone as a "recorder" within 30cm of the device. Sync in post.

**Sync trick:**
**Clap once** at the start of every take (a manual clapperboard). In your editor, align the sharp "clap" waveform across all tracks.

## Step 5: Shoot 60-Second Test (45-55 min)

Validate the full rig with something simple — pop a phone case, swap a keycap, replace a fan.

**Shot checklist:**

```text
□ Overhead cam REC
□ Detail cam REC
□ Independent mic REC
□ Clap for sync
□ State the goal (1 sentence)
□ Show initial state (3-second static shot)
□ Execute the disassembly (30-40 seconds)
□ Show final state (3-second static shot)
□ Clap to end
```

**Action principles while shooting:**

- **Slow down 30%** from your natural pace — viewers need time to see
- **Pause 1 second** after each key action (remove screw → pause → grab next)
- **Stabilize hands** — rest elbows on the desk
- Look at **your hands**, not the camera (unless doing pieces to camera)

**Import to DaVinci Resolve / Final Cut / CapCut:**

1. Drop all three tracks (overhead + detail + audio) onto the timeline
2. Align using the clap waveform
3. Use overhead as the primary; cut to detail cam for 2-4 seconds on key moments
4. Mute camera-internal audio, use the independent mic track

<Checkpoint>
Play back the test — can a viewer understand **what you're disassembling** and **how you're doing it**?
<Answer>Yes, overhead shows the whole picture, detail cam reveals hand precision, audio conveys the mechanical feedback</Answer>
</Checkpoint>

## Step 6: Save Your Rig Preset (55-60 min)

Lock down this setup so next time you're rolling in 5 minutes, not 60.

**Preset checklist:**

- 📏 Mark the **overhead arm height** with tape on the pole
- 📐 Mark the **light stand positions** with tape or floor markers
- 🎛 Screenshot your **camera settings** (ISO / shutter / WB)
- 📸 Take a wide shot of the whole **lighting setup** — save it to a "Rig Presets" album
- 📝 Note focal length, aperture, focus distance in a memo

**Next-time shoot workflow:**

```text
1. Position lights using the reference photo    → 30 sec
2. Extend arm to marked height                  → 30 sec
3. Load camera settings from screenshot         → 1 min
4. Mic on, sync-check                           → 1 min
5. Clap and roll                                → instant flow
```

🎉 In one hour, you've built the same rig used by iFixit, hardware YouTubers, and repair channels. The rest is teardown volume and edit speed.

## 🎁 Bonus

- **Add a third angle**: 45° side-view for the presenter or wide establishing shots
- **Macro attachment**: Clip-on macro lens ($5-20) for PCB traces and micro components
- **Underlit glass**: Backlit glass for transparent devices (watches, camera lenses) — pro trick
- **Multi-track audio**: Record lav + shotgun simultaneously, pick the best in post
- **Green screen**: Small chroma sheet behind the workspace for "floating part" reveals
